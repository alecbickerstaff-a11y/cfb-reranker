How q and z work

Pick a team to walk through its numbers from raw game results to final score.

Tap a team to open its game log. Tap a column header to sort. Opp str is the average adjusted strength of every opponent played.

Opponent strength (q)

The opponent's win percentage with the game against this team removed.

Then multiplied by the opponent's conference value from the Conferences tab. Defaults: SEC, Big Ten, Big 12, ACC and Notre Dame 1.00, every other FBS conference 0.70, FCS opponents fixed at 0.10.

H2H game value

Win: 1 + q, plus 0.10 on the road or 0.05 at a neutral site.

Loss: q − 0.5, minus 0.10 at home, plus 0.10 if it was within 7 points.

H2H raw is the average across all games, so 7 and 8 game teams compare evenly. With recency above 0, it's a weighted average: last week's game counts 100% and a week 1 game counts 100 − 0.8 × recency percent.

Offense adj

Per game: min(points, 56) × (0.6 + 0.8q), then averaged.

Scoring 30 on a q of 0.9 counts as 40. Scoring 30 on a q of 0.1 counts as 20.

Defense adj

Per game: points allowed × (1.4 − 0.8q), then averaged. Lower is better.

Allowing 20 to a q of 0.1 counts as 26. Allowing 20 to a q of 0.9 counts as 14.

z-scores and the final score

Each stat is converted to a z-score across every tracked team, so 0 is the group average and ±1 is one standard deviation.

Score = 50 + 15 × (wH·zH + wO·zO − wD·zD) with the weights rescaled to sum to 1.

Opponent strength by conference

Each opponent's win percentage is multiplied by its conference's value to get q. 1.00 takes a record at face value. At 0.70, a 6-1 record there counts about like a 4-3 record in a 1.00 conference. Values above 1.00 rate that conference's records as harder to earn than face value.

The AP's implied weights

For each poll, every mix of H2H, offense, defense and preseason reputation in 5% steps (1,770 mixes), at five recency settings and 33 conference settings (Group of 5 value 0 to 1.0, Big 12 and ACC value 0.8 to 1.0), is scored against that week's AP points, and the best fit is kept. Each team's stats use only games played before that poll. This is the AP's ballot translated into your four sliders.

Fit r is the Pearson correlation between the weighted score and AP points, among the tracked teams ranked that week. Every team ranked at any point this season is tracked, and each poll is fit on the tracked teams ranked that week (the Teams column). Opponent strength uses each opponent's current record, so early-week q values carry some hindsight. Early polls fit almost perfectly because the AP had barely moved off its preseason order.

Agreement with the AP poll

Three correlation measures between the résumé model at your current weights and the Oct 25 AP poll. Pearson uses the raw scores and AP points. Spearman and Kendall use only the order. Kendall τ-b corrects for ties, including every unranked team sharing rank 26. Unranked teams use their others-receiving-votes points where they have them.

Season history

AP rank for every tracked team across every poll since preseason. NR means unranked that week. Labels show this week's top 25; pick any team, ranked or not, to trace it.

Poll inertia

How much each AP poll is a copy of the last one. Spearman ρ between consecutive polls, average rank movement, and how fast the preseason order is fading. Unranked teams are scored 26.

Preseason anchoring

Does preseason position still drive the AP order after you account for results? The partial correlation removes the part of the AP order that the résumé model already explains. It updates with the sliders.

Correlation matrix

Every input against every other. Blue is positive, red is negative, and * marks p < .05. Defense is flipped so higher is better, and preseason is flipped so a higher value means a better preseason rank.

Preseason ranked vs unranked

Which tracked teams were in the preseason poll, split by top 12 and everyone else in each ranking. A low p-value means preseason status and current placement are linked. This is a chi-square style test of independence, run as Fisher's exact test because several cells have fewer than 5 teams and the chi-square approximation breaks down there.

Weight sensitivity

Every combination of the three sliders in steps of 10 (1,330 settings), with the tiebreak set as it is now. The line is each team's best to worst rank, the box is the middle half, the dot is your current weights and the red tick is the AP rank. Short lines are teams whose spot doesn't depend on how you weight things.

Pearson r

Linear relationship between two continuous variables. Used for résumé score against AP points and in the matrix.

Spearman ρ

Pearson on ranks. Used wherever the data is an ordering, and as the robust version in the matrix since AP points are skewed.

Kendall τ-b

Share of team pairs both rankings put in the same order, corrected for ties. The cleanest read on ranking agreement with a small sample.

Fisher's exact

Stands in for chi-square on the preseason table, since the expected cell counts are under 5.

AR(1) and lag correlation

The time series piece. Nine polls is too short for ARIMA, VAR or Granger, which need dozens of periods. An AR(1) slope pooled across team-weeks and week-over-week ρ measure the same thing those models would: how much the poll depends on itself.
